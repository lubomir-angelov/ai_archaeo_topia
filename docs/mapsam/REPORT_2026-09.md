# MapSAM detection and segmentation — complete report, September 2026

Covers the v0.5 detector work, the ground-truth error it exposed, the v0.4
revision that error required, and the first end-to-end measurement of the full
pipeline. Per-version detail lives in `v005/RESULTS.md` and
`v004/RESULTS_REVISION.md`; this document is the synthesis.

**One-line status.** A map sheet now goes in and archaeologically usable mound
masks come out: 0.958 macro rate at mask IoU ≥ 0.5, with localization
comfortably inside the segmenter's working band. The binding constraint is no
longer any model — it is the amount and correctness of annotated data.

---

## 1. What changed this cycle

v0.1–v0.4 answered *"given the correct mound location, can MapSAM segment it?"*
— yes, 0.79 macro IoU with 99–100% of instances clearing IoU 0.5. Nothing had
attempted *"can the system find mounds?"*

Four things happened:

1. **A detector was built** and cleared its localization target on the first
   attempt.
2. **Its one failing sheet turned out to be an annotation error**, not a model
   failure. Twelve symbols labelled `mound` are mills.
3. **v0.4 was re-run** against corrected labels. Its conclusions survive.
4. **The pipeline was closed end to end** for the first time.

---

## 2. The specification, corrected

v0.4 summarized its prompt-jitter finding as "the prompt must land within
~15 px". That reading was wrong and was corrected at the start of v0.5. The
deployment-relevant arm — prompt and window moving together, as a
detector-driven crop does — reads:

| prompt offset | disagreement area px² | IoU≥0.5 rate |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 91.8 | 1.00 |
| 5 | 140.5 | 1.00 |
| 10 | 255.0 | 0.69 |
| 15 | 444.6 | **0.02** |

15 px is where the segmenter *fails*. The working band is ≤5 px. So the
detector target became **p90 centre error ≤ 5 source px, primary metric
recall@5px** — not mAP, and not recall at a loose radius.

This choice is the reason everything else in this report was legible. It is
also what made the annotation error findable: it kept recall and localization
as separate axes instead of averaging them into one score.

## 3. The detector

YOLO26-s, one class, 512 px windows at stride 384, leave-one-sheet-out on the
v0.4 folds, 150 epochs, `last.pt` with no checkpoint selection (the only
validation split available is the held-out sheet).

Recall@5px at confidence 0.05, before and after the label correction:

| fold | eval sheet | v0.0.1 labels | v0.0.2 labels | p90 error |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | K-35-51-B-a | 0.859 (110/128) | 0.898 (115/128) | 3.5 px |
| B | K-35-8-G-a | 1.000 (34/34) | 1.000 (34/34) | 3.7 px |
| C | K-34-35-B-g | 0.389 (7/18) | **1.000 (7/7)** | 2.0 px |
| | macro | 0.749 | **0.966** | |

**The recall curve is flat in radius on every fold of both runs** —
recall@5px equals recall@25px. There is no near-miss population: the detector
emits correct points or nothing. Two consequences follow immediately.
Localization is solved and further work on it would optimize nothing. And
step 7 of the plan, prompt-jitter augmentation to widen the segmenter's
tolerance, is not merely untriggered but contraindicated.

The non-neural reference makes the gap concrete. Template matching
(`cv2.matchTemplate`, k-means templates from training sheets) reaches
comparable recall only at 55–74 false positives per window; at a rate a
reviewer could absorb it manages 0.15–0.45. YOLO26-s reaches 1.000 on two
sheets at 0.00–0.02 FP per window.

## 4. The ground-truth error

Experiment 1's fold C scored 0.389. Reviewing those failures, the annotator
found that twelve symbols on `K-34-35-B-g` are mills — confirmed with a second
project annotator — and that one genuine mound was unlabelled.

**Eleven of the twelve mislabels are exactly the annotations the detector
refused to fire on.** The eight on one clip lie in a narrow corridor, x
1500–1651 across y 574–1103, which is a line of mills along a road and not a
scatter of burial mounds.

**The unlabelled mound had already been found.** It sits at (1771, 813); the
detector had placed a candidate at (1772.5, 812.0) — 1.8 px away, confidence
0.586, the second-strongest detection on the sheet — and it was scored as a
false positive.

So on that sheet the evaluation charged the model eleven false negatives for
correctly rejecting mills and one false positive for correctly finding a mound.

### Why this is a result and not an anecdote

**The metric design localized it.** Fold C had the *best* p90 of the three
folds, 2.00 px, alongside the worst recall, 0.389. A detector genuinely failing
on a sheet degrades on both axes. A single aggregate score would have shown one
mediocre number and given no reason to look at the annotations.

**It reproduces under an unrelated method.** The template baseline, which
shares no code path with YOLO and has no capacity to overfit, moves 0.389 →
0.857 on the same sheet under the same correction.

**A domain rule predicts it.** A mound cannot be crossed by a *surface*
watercourse — water runs along terrain lows, a mound is raised — and all twelve
mills carry `crossed_by_water_line`. Water mills sit on watercourses, so the
attribute was true of them and it is mounds that the water excludes.

Applying it to the rest of the corrected export flagged two more, and reviewing
those taught the rule its limits: one is a real mound whose attribute was set
in error, and the other is a real mound with a genuine water line crossing it
**underground**. An underground line is a pipe and can run beneath anything.
The attribute does not distinguish surface from underground, so this is a
review hint, not a mechanical check — a hit means a mislabelled symbol, a
wrongly set attribute, or a legitimate underground crossing. Splitting the
attribute into surface and underground variants is the cheapest schema change
available with the largest validation payoff.

### A first retraction

The original write-up offered a hypothesis: that fold C's failures concentrated
on mounds lacking a companion height glyph, which appeared to corroborate an
unexplained directional anisotropy from v0.4. **That is withdrawn.** Mills sit
along roads and carry no mound height marks, so "no height mark, crossed by a
road" described the mislabelled class, not a hard visual case. v0.4's anisotropy
is unexplained again.

## 5. The v0.4 revision

Twelve of v0.4's 171 training samples stopped being mounds, so its step 3 arm
was re-run against the corrected annotations. Configs identical but for the
dataset root.

The fold structure isolates two questions, because the corrected sheet is a
*training* sheet for folds A and B and the *evaluation* sheet for fold C:

| fold | train | eval | changed | IoU v0.4 → v0.4r |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 51 → 40 | 120 → 120 | training only | 0.7930 → 0.7913 |
| B | 137 → 126 | 34 → 34 | training only | 0.8261 → 0.8288 |
| C | 154 → 154 | 17 → 6 | evaluation only | 0.7574 → 0.8137 |
| | | | **macro** | **0.7922 → 0.8113** |

**Training-label noise cost segmentation nothing measurable.** Folds A and B
moved −0.0017 and +0.0027 with their evaluation sets untouched — inside normal
run-to-run variation.

**Fold C's gain is an evaluation-population change, not better segmentation.**
Its training set is byte-identical between runs. What changed is that eleven
small symbols left its evaluation set and the mean ground-truth area rose
381.2 → 550.0 source px². v0.3 established that smaller symbols segment worse.

### The structural reason only the detector could see the error

> **MapSAM is a conditional segmenter and does not decide semantics.** Given a
> prompt on a printed symbol it outlines that symbol, and a mill is a printed
> symbol. The mislabels cost v0.1–v0.4 almost nothing and were invisible to
> every metric those versions reported. It took the detector — the first stage
> that must decide *whether* something is a mound — to expose them.

A conditional segmenter cannot audit its own labels. A detector can. That is a
property of the architecture, not of this dataset.

## 6. End to end

Detector candidates at confidence 0.25 → prompts → v0.4r decoder → masks scored
against each annotation's geometry.

| fold | sheet | n | IoU≥0.5 | IoU≥0.75 | mean IoU | missed | false masks |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | K-35-51-B-a | 128 | 0.875 | 0.586 | 0.6820 | 0.109 | 60 |
| B | K-35-8-G-a | 34 | 1.000 | 0.529 | 0.7482 | 0.000 | 0 |
| C | K-34-35-B-g | 7 | 1.000 | 0.857 | 0.7717 | 0.000 | 3 |

Macro IoU≥0.5: **0.958**.

**The end-to-end loss is detector recall and essentially nothing else.** Fold
A's missed rate is 0.109 against a detector miss rate of 0.117 at the same
threshold. Restricting to mounds the detector found:

| fold | mean IoU, found only | v0.4r with ground-truth prompt | prompt cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 0.7657 | 0.7913 | −0.026 |
| B | 0.7482 | 0.8288 | −0.081 |
| C | 0.7717 | 0.8137 | −0.042 |

Fold B is the clean case: 34 of 34 found, so no recall loss at all, isolating
the cost of a detector-derived prompt at 0.081 IoU with p90 3.66 px. Real but
modest, and in the direction v0.4's tolerance curve predicts.

**Box scale costs mask precision, not mask detection.** Prompting with a
canonical median-sized box on the detector's point beats prompting with the
detector's own box at IoU≥0.75 on all three folds (+0.016, +0.059, +0.286),
while the two are within 0.006 at IoU≥0.5 and in mean IoU. The direction is
consistent 3 of 3 but the magnitudes are weak — fold A's +0.016 is two
instances of 128 — so treat it as a cheap default worth adopting rather than an
established effect. This is the box-scale question v0.4 explicitly left
untested.

## 7. What this is scoped to

**Three sheets of one Bulgarian 1:25k series, leave-one-sheet-out, no frozen test
split.** All of it is development evidence, not a test result. Specifically not
established:

- **That these numbers generalize.** Three sheets, one series. Fold C now
  contributes seven evaluation mounds and its interval is 0.646–1.000.
- **That the corrected labels are complete.** One adversarial review of one
  sheet found twelve errors and one omission, and the water-line rule then
  surfaced one further attribute error on another sheet. Neither of the other
  two sheets has had an equivalent adversarial pass, and attribute errors of
  this kind are invisible to every metric reported here.
- **That experiment 2's gain is attributable only to the correction** on fold
  A. Its +3.9 points sits inside its own checkpoint-to-checkpoint spread
  (0.664–0.891).
- **Any false-positive rate at sheet scale.** The 12 clips were selected for
  annotation, so rates are reported per window and per megapixel and labelled
  as measured on annotation-selected clips.
- **That YOLO26-s is the right architecture.** First one tried; it cleared the
  target immediately, and the flat recall curve suggests remaining headroom is
  not in the box regressor.

### A methodological result worth keeping

Fold A's recall swings ±11 points between saved checkpoints with no trend, as
wide as its confidence interval. Evaluating `last.pt` rather than `best.pt`
avoided reporting 0.891 instead of 0.812 — a measurement of exactly how large
the leak would have been, and the second concrete argument for a permanent
validation split.

## 8. What to do next

**First, and it now blocks everything: more sheets.** Every model-side target
in the plan is met, and nothing further about robustness can be learned from
three sheets. Sixty more sheets of the same series have since been acquired;
`v006/PLAN.md` covers ingesting them and, more importantly, building the
project's first frozen test set from a blind-annotated slice of them. A second
cartographic series is still needed separately — sixty more sheets of the same
series cannot retire the cross-cartographic caveat.

**Second, review the detector's false positives as a queue, not an error rate.**
One of them was already a real mound. Fold A has 60 false masks at confidence
0.25; the ones landing on `background` have never been inspected. This is the
cheapest available source of new labelled data and of further annotation
corrections.

**Third, run the water-line rule over every future export** before training on
it, treating a hit as "look at this annotation" rather than "relabel it", and
consider splitting `crossed_by_water_line` into surface and underground
variants so the check can be mechanical.

Not worth doing: prompt-jitter augmentation (contraindicated), a second-stage
classifier (0.00–0.36 false positives per window leaves nothing to filter),
further localization work (flat recall curve), and architecture comparisons
against Faster R-CNN or RT-DETR until there is a failure they could address.

Deferred with reasons in `v005/PLAN.md`: calibration, the segmentation-quality
gate, attribute extraction, sub-512 windows, encoder unfreezing.
